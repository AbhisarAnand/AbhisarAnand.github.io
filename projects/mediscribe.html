The problem

Make unusual patterns visible for review

The capstone explores healthcare data workflows through transcription, dashboards, and analysis. One part of the work focuses on making cross-hospital anomalies visible to a reviewer.

My contribution

I contributed cross-hospital anomaly detection and hospital alerts to the team’s MediScribe capstone. My work focused on surfacing unusual patterns for review within the larger healthcare application.

From input to outcome

How it works

The C# service groups symptom and diagnosis records by location, compares recent activity with a historical baseline, and routes findings through hospital notifications. Findings include counts, a category and an explanation, with scan results and failures recorded in logs.

  1. 01

    Gather the relevant records

    The service joins symptoms or diagnoses to visits and groups the records by location for comparison.

  2. 02

    Compare recent activity

    The spike detector compares recent 24-hour symptom counts with daily activity in the preceding baseline window.

  3. 03

    Describe the finding

    Findings carry a category, recent count, baseline and explanation rather than only returning a yes-or-no flag.

  4. 04

    Notify reviewers

    The service passes detected anomalies to the hospital notification workflow and records scan activity in logs.

Behind the implementation

Design decisions

Keep the alert explainable

The implementation distinguishes novel symptoms, statistical spikes and diagnosis clusters. A reviewer can see what type of pattern triggered the alert and the supporting counts.

Keep the component’s role clear

I contributed the anomaly detection and alerts within a larger team application. These are review signals in an academic prototype, not a diagnosis or a clinically validated recommendation.

Project context

This was an academic team project, separate from my MIE internship. The alerts were not clinically validated.
